The Water Cycle

A journey through the air, land, and living world

Water is always moving. It travels from the ocean to the atmosphere, falls onto land, and eventually finds its way back to the sea. This continuous journey is called the water cycle.

4.1 Water changes and moves

The Sun provides energy that drives much of the cycle. Water changes between liquid, gas, and solid states as it moves through the environment. As you read, the small visual companion in the corner follows the section headings and adds a picture to each idea.

Evaporation: water rises

When sunlight warms water in oceans, lakes, and rivers, some liquid water changes into an invisible gas called water vapor. The vapor rises into the air. Plants also release water vapor through a process called transpiration.

Together, evaporation and transpiration move water from Earth's surface into the atmosphere. On a warm day, more water may evaporate; on a cool day, less does.

Condensation: clouds form

Higher in the atmosphere, air is cooler. Water vapor cools and changes back into tiny liquid droplets. This change is condensation. The droplets gather around tiny particles in the air and form clouds.

Clouds are made of countless droplets or ice crystals. Wind carries them across the sky, sometimes far from the place where their water first evaporated.

Precipitation: water returns

As cloud droplets collide, they grow. When they become too heavy to stay suspended, water falls as precipitation. It may arrive as rain, snow, sleet, or hail, depending on the temperature of the air.

Precipitation replenishes streams, lakes, and soil. Snow can store water until warmer weather melts it.

4.2 Water gathers and begins again

Collection and runoff: the journey continues

Some water soaks into the ground and replenishes groundwater. Other water flows downhill as runoff, joining streams and rivers that carry it toward the ocean. From there, the Sun can warm it again, and the cycle continues.

Water may take days or thousands of years to complete one journey. Wherever it goes, it connects the atmosphere, land, and living things.
